1. **Where does the conclusion fail?**  
   The draft treats resumability as sufficient to make a long run viable without establishing a total runtime budget or reducing the failure exposure of the 49-step chain. A 6 h press cap alone does not establish that the remaining work can finish reliably. Its ordering also puts press optimization ahead of the six audits already known to prevent COMPLETE, without explaining that dependency. “Days since last COMPLETE” is ambiguous once COMPLETE_WITH_FILED_FAILURES exists: resetting the metric could conceal degradation; excluding that outcome could conceal successful degraded publication. Finally, changing scheduler settings enables unattended launches but does not resolve the reported shutdown causes or prove that credentials and dependencies work when logged out.

2. **What material failure mode is missing?**  
   **Individually atomic files can still form an inconsistent published dataset.** Temp-then-rename does not make a step’s multiple outputs, ledger update, and publication pointer one transaction. A crash between those operations can leave mixed generations, duplicate publication, or a ledger that certifies artifacts it cannot reproduce. A kill-mid-write byte comparison misses these commit-boundary failures unless explicitly tested.

   The freshness rule also confuses *recent* with *complete and mutually consistent*. A collector can produce a fresh but truncated dataset; cached LLM classifications can omit new evidence; a zero-row member result can indicate an authentication or parser failure. Every source might satisfy its age window while the ranking remains materially wrong. Pending coverage, valid empty results, and shared input generations need treatment alongside vintage.

3. **Was the strongest opposing view suppressed?**  
   Yes, materially. The skeptic’s strongest alternative was short, independently resumable source collections feeding a fast compute/publish cycle, with collection moved off the unreliable laptop. The draft borrows implementation safeguards but retains the long shared workflow without evaluating that alternative. It also omits the skeptic’s hard total time budget. The critic’s strongest publication concern is only partly preserved: per-source age limits do not necessarily ensure that evidence represents a coherent common period or that compute consumed the validated source versions.

4. **Would you sign off? If not, why?**  
   No. The draft leaves the central correctness contract undefined: what constitutes committed step completion, which exact inputs a publication certifies, how incomplete coverage affects eligibility, and how degraded outcomes interact with supervision and the metric. “Fix the five defects” names necessary work but supplies no acceptance criteria. Sign-off would require those criteria and evidence covering interrupted commits, clock changes and concurrent writers, unattended startup, and publication from partial or mixed-generation inputs.
