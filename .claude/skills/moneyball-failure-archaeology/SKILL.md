# Moneyball failure archaeology

This is the project's institutional memory of everything that already went wrong, was already tried, or was already decided — kept so nobody re-fights a settled battle. The Moneyball system (a Python pipeline in `fec/` that ranks 533 members of Congress by persuadability for a credit-union lobbying effort) accumulated nine audit reports, ~20 preregistered experiments, and dozens of closed investigations in its first week of git history (2026-07-02..07-09). Most defects here are "silent wrong-number" — the pipeline prints success while producing subtly wrong output — so the record below, not intuition, is what tells you whether a symptom is a known non-defect, a fixed regression, or genuinely new. Before you flag, fix, propose, or delete anything: search this file for it first.

## When NOT to use this skill

- You have a **live failure right now** and need to diagnose it → `moneyball-debugging-playbook` (symptom→triage). Come back here to check whether your diagnosis matches a known incident.
- You need the **rules** for what changes are allowed and who decides → `moneyball-change-control` (owns the gating vocabulary and the non-negotiables master list).
- You want to **run a new experiment** or understand prereg/look/battery discipline → `moneyball-research-methodology`.
- You need to know what may be **claimed in writing** (validated-claims ledger, exact caveats) → `moneyball-docs-and-claims`.
- You want the **analysis techniques** these post-mortems used (power studies, byte-match purity proofs) → `moneyball-proof-and-analysis-toolkit`.

## 2. The research kill-ledger — closed/retired validation routes
