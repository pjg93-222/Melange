# Mentat: top-down methodology review

September 10, 2026. Read-only review of the current implementation and previous audit. These are findings and proposed principles, not additional fixes already applied. Scope remains FEC political giving, excluding lobbying.

The central remaining weakness is that source collection, attribution, economic accounting and presentation do not yet consistently share the same definition of the number. Correct arithmetic and complete metadata cannot establish that the number means what its label says.

## Priorities and evidence

| Priority | General principle | Finding and consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Define the population before claiming completeness. | Completing every roster query proves collection of that query universe, not all company or industry giving. The report calls the selected-company denominator “Total Industry Spending.” Use “share of tracked companies” unless whole-industry coverage is established. Federal/FEC scope, dates, source products and attribution limitations must be explicit. |
| 2 | Preserve source evidence independently of its current interpretation. | Completed API page checkpoints are deleted in `R/fec_api.R:756–770`; the normalized receipt projection in `R/collect.R:1164–1205` omits `contributor_id`. Retaining selected database columns is not a complete raw-response archive. Archive full responses, request context and checksums before discarding temporary pages; replay classification from that archive. |
| 3 | Company attribution must be dated and independent of collection order. | Company matching inputs do not supply effective dates to the matcher (`R/collect.R:378–440`). The insertion path retains the first stored company and logs some alternate-company matches separately (`R/collect.R:1244–1285`). Preserve all claims, centrally resolve company/PAC/employee relationships for the relevant date, and carry unresolved attribution explicitly. A new acquisition must not silently rewrite historical ownership. |
| 4 | Give each economic gift an identity distinct from its filing rows. | Production retains amendment/intermediary stages; the experiment selects only among observed filing evidence (`tools/original_giving_experiment.py:45–70`). A full event ledger needs the original gift, later representations, corrections and route allocations. Verify complete amendment families, including omitted/deleted transactions. Partial and many-to-many transfers require dollar allocations, not only one-to-one duplicate flags. |
| 5 | Preserve amount, timing and ownership through adjustments. | Negative receipt amounts are called refunds (`R/collect.R:1224`), but negative corrections and actual donor refunds are different events. Keep gross original giving, linked refunds/corrections and net giving separate. Define how refunds crossing cycles are shown. Keep donation date, filing/amendment date, observation date and publication cutoff distinct. Old cycles can change when later amendments arrive. |
| 6 | Let the categories determine the report layout. | `R/report.R:17–25` groups leadership PACs into “Company PAC” and parties/JFCs/unclassified recipients into “Candidate.” Broad `CORPORATE` record buckets still drive corporate headline metrics (`R/report.R:554–570`). Correctly summed columns can still misdescribe the money. Preserve distinct recipient types and distinguish actual treasury ownership from organization-name matches. |
| 7 | Report uncertainty in the meaning of dollars, not just metadata counts. | The earlier reduction to ten missing form/line records does not establish identity, uniqueness or route completeness. Track collection, ownership, transaction meaning and linkage separately, by records and affected dollars. Unresolved amounts belong in a separate review view; they are neither zero nor verified original giving. Overlapping uncertain representations are not an error bound. |
| 8 | Make percentages meaningful. | `R/report.R:223–250` and `:539–557` handle a zero partisan denominator but do not prevent signed amounts yielding misleading shares: R = −$100 and D = $50 produces 200% Republican. Specify the denominator and classified coverage, show refunds separately, and suppress misleading percentages. Do not cap a bad ratio into an apparently valid range. |
| 9 | Validate decisions independently and keep the explanation synchronized. | Source guards check preservation and decision consistency, not independent truth. Use original-filing adjudication on both large-dollar cases and a representative random sample; measure false additions and false removals separately. The generated method note at `R/report.R:2844` still describes the obsolete resemblance-based memo rule. Version and test explanatory text alongside calculations. |

The code also uses `INSERT OR IGNORE` plus fill-blank metadata updates (`R/collect.R:1282–1285,1335–1355`). A conflicting later observation for an existing source ID should create an explicit source-conflict record rather than be silently ignored. This is a demonstrated path that lacks a conflict check, not a claim that an amount change was observed during this review.

## How the process should work

1. **Define the question.** List the companies, historical subsidiaries/PACs, dates, source scope and accounting basis. The approved basis is original employee and corporate giving once, with PAC outgoing activity separately.
2. **Collect and preserve evidence.** Query the FEC, verify every requested scope, save complete responses and record what is unavailable. Reuse cached evidence; fetch changed filings and missing relationships rather than recollecting everything after a rule change.
3. **Identify the parties and the event.** Determine who supplied the funds, who received them, which company relationship is supported, and whether the record is a gift, transfer, refund, correction, interest payment or asset sale.
4. **Connect records describing the same money.** Resolve filing versions, conduits, joint fundraising, asset conversions and refunds. Assign one original-gift identity and preserve every source record and decision behind it. Route allocations cannot exceed the amount of their source gift; do not infer an exact donor share of pooled PAC spending without evidence.
5. **Calculate separate views.** Show employee giving, supported corporate treasury giving, gross/refunds/net, recipient types, and PAC outgoing activity. A parent-company rollup and its subsidiaries must not both be added to the same grand total. Count a gift once even if its allocation is displayed across multiple recipients.
6. **Check and publish.** Reconcile signed changes in cents, test classifications against independent original-filing examples, show remaining uncertainty and publish a dated version. Compare OpenSecrets only after matching scope and accounting basis; explain differences at the record level.

For example, an employee contributes $100 to a company PAC and the PAC later contributes $60 to a candidate. The original-giving view records $100 employee giving; the PAC-spending view records $60 outgoing. They are two views of overlapping money and must not produce a $160 original-giving headline. The remaining $40 is not assumed to have reached a candidate.

If the PAC instead forwards one earmarked $100 gift through an intermediary, multiple filings may describe it. A supported route lets us show the ultimate recipient while counting $100 once. Without sufficient evidence, show the unresolved route rather than guessing the recipient or declaring another $100 gift.

## Independent checks and practical limits

The FEC itself defines different data products differently. Its [individual-contributions bulk file](https://www.fec.gov/campaign-finance-data/contributions-individuals-file-description/) is a subset of itemized individual contributions and has inclusion thresholds; it must not silently substitute for the entire Schedule A universe. That documentation also distinguishes a transaction identifier from a unique filing-row identifier and explains why memo flags alone do not establish duplicate gifts.

Where full committee reporting is collected, reconcile comparable receipt/disbursement totals and beginning/ending balances with the corresponding filing summaries. Do not force an employer-filtered subset to equal an entire committee's total. The FEC's [committee report summaries](https://www.fec.gov/campaign-finance-data/committee-report-summary-file-metadata/) incorporate amendments and can cover different reporting periods, so reconciliation requires matched periods and versions.

A successful independent check demonstrates specific properties under a defined scope. It does not make missing disclosures observable or establish undocumented company relationships. Being better than an external summary means more defensible coverage, classification, linkage and explanations—not automatically a bigger number or a closer match.
